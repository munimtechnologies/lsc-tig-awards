# TIG 2018-2020, and LSC's own counts checked against its lists

The main README covers LSC's Technology Initiative Grant (TIG) rounds from 2021 to 2025. This page adds the three rounds before them, 2018, 2019 and 2020, from LSC's per-year project description pages, and checks them against LSC's press releases for the same rounds. It then checks every count of grants, organizations and states that LSC printed for 2018-2025 against the grants it listed.

`data/tig_history.json` holds the 82 grants of the three rounds as the description pages print them (grantee, state, TIG number, amount), every amount another LSC release prints differently, and 20 statements by LSC quoted with their source. Each list was read from the raw text of LSC's pages and summed by hand, and the tests check the sums.

- **2018: three LSC documents disagree, and one combination of their figures gives the stated total.** On 15 October 2018 LSC published two releases. The [full release](https://www.lsc.gov/press-release/lsc-awards-nearly-4-million-technology-grants-legal-aid-organizations-0) lists all 26 grants and prints Northwest Justice Project's grant as $267,175. The [description page](https://www.lsc.gov/grants/technology-initiative-grant-program/technology-initiative-grant-awards-tig-projects-funded-year/2018-tig-awards-project-descriptions) prints $297,175. A [second release](https://www.lsc.gov/press-release/2018-technology-initiative-grants-awarded-24-lsc-grantees) bundles the announcements for nine grantees and prints Alaska Legal Services Corporation's grant as $171,879, in its heading and again in its text. The page and the full release both print $179,879. Neither full list matches the stated $3,884,257: the page is $8,000 over and the full release $22,000 under. The page's list with Alaska at $171,879 matches it to the dollar, and no other single change in the table does. So either the page and the full release both carry the same wrong Alaska figure (and the full release a second error for Northwest Justice Project), or the stated total is itself off. Each pair of disputed figures differs in a single digit, which looks like typing errors rather than changed awards, but the documents can't settle which figure LSC paid. The same per-grantee release prints Legal Assistance Foundation's grant as $266,000 in its heading and as a "$264,000 grant" later in the same announcement.
- **2019: neither list matches the stated total, and no single correction makes it match.** The page's list is $684 over the stated $4,230,718, and the release's list, which prints South Carolina Legal Services' grant $10 lower, is $674 over. None of the 30 amounts is $684, so one dropped grant doesn't explain it. The page also prints TIG #19044 for two different grants and no number for Atlanta Legal Aid Society's.
- **2020 matches exactly.** The release prints two amounts $1 away from the page's, in opposite directions, so both lists sum to the stated $3,941,298. The release's "three grants totally $399,045" for Michigan Advocacy Program is the sum of its own three figures.

## A fourth 2018 list: LawNext, November 2018 (not an LSC document)

On 9 November 2018 LawNext (LawSites) published [the 2018 round](https://www.lawnext.com/2018/11/latest-round-lsc-tig-grants-show-tech-can-help-expand-access-justice.html) with every grant's amount and description, "via LSC". `data/tig_2018_lawnext.json` holds its 26 amounts, read from the raw text of the article and matched to LSC's TIG numbers. It is a third-party copy, so nothing here treats its figures as LSC's.

| Grantee | TIG # | LawNext | Description page | LawNext minus page | Other LSC source | It prints | LawNext minus it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Northwest Justice Project | 18008 | $266,000 | $297,175 | -$31,175 | release | $267,175 | -$1,175 |
| Legal Assistance Foundation | 18010 | $264,000 | $266,000 | -$2,000 | per-grantee release | $264,000 | $0 |
| Alaska Legal Services Corporation | 18046 | $169,879 | $179,879 | -$10,000 | per-grantee release | $171,879 | -$2,000 |
| Ohio State Legal Services | 18056 | $783,200 | $783,000 | +$200 | - | - | - |

Generated by `python lawnext_2018.py` (`--summary` for the figures below), and checked by the tests. The table shows only the grants that are off the $2,000 gap or that LSC prints twice.

- **23 of LawNext's 26 amounts are exactly $2,000 below LSC's description page**, from Legal Services Alabama ($24,999 against $26,999) to Utah Legal Services ($306,047 against $308,047). A uniform gap across grants of every size looks like one systematic difference between the list LawNext copied and the one LSC posted, not typing errors. LSC's documents don't explain it.
- **LawNext's Alaska figure fits the per-grantee release, not the page.** $169,879 is $2,000 below the per-grantee release's $171,879, the same gap as the 23 others, and $10,000 below the page's $179,879. So a fourth document is consistent with $171,879, the one figure that makes the page's list equal LSC's stated total.
- **Legal Assistance Foundation's "$264,000 grant"** in the body of the per-grantee release is exactly LawNext's figure, while its heading and the other LSC documents print $266,000. That release's body may have kept a figure from the same earlier list.
- **Two grants don't fit the gap.** Ohio State Legal Services is $200 above the page ($783,200 against $783,000), and Northwest Justice Project ($266,000) is below both of LSC's figures ($297,175 and $267,175).
- **LawNext's list adds up to $3,805,282, while its text says "$3,891,457".** That total is $86,175 above LawNext's own list and $7,200 above LSC's stated $3,884,257. We found no combination of the figures that explains it.

They did not come from LSC's later move to new addresses.
- **No archived LSC document prints LawNext's $3,891,457 or its $2,000-lower amounts.** The only LawNext figure in them is Legal Assistance Foundation's "$264,000 grant" in the body of the per-grantee release, the same as today. So the list LawNext copied is still not found in LSC's public pages.
- **The two releases of 15 October 2018 give different running totals for the program.** The full release says "LSC has funded more than 700 technology projects totally more than $60 million"; the per-grantee release says "more than 720 technology projects at nearly $65 million". Both read the same in the archived copies and today.

- **All 11 counts of grants LSC printed fit its lists. Eight of its 20 counts of organizations or states do not.**

The index page's "22 legal services organizations" for 2025 can't be the organization count either: the list has 29.

## What this does and doesn't say

- These are checks of LSC's documents against each other. Where they disagree, the arithmetic shows which figures are consistent with LSC's stated totals. It does not show what LSC actually paid; only LSC's grant records can.
- LawNext's list is a journalist's copy, not an LSC record. It is used only as a fourth reading of the same round, and its amounts never replace LSC's in any table or total. We have not found the earlier LSC list it may have been copied from, in LSC's pages today or in the archived copies.
- The README's TIG tables still cover 2021-2025. The 2018-2020 rounds are here for the checks and are not in `data/awards.json`, and they carry no AI or intake flags.
- Each grantee's state was read from its name and project description and checked twice. The 2019 American Samoa grant is a territory and counts in the states column.
- The 2018-2020 pages and releases were fetched on 17 October 2026. For 2018, the Internet Archive's copies from December 2018 and June 2019 print the same amounts and stated total. The earliest of them is from almost eight weeks after the announcement, so a change made in those first weeks can't be ruled out. The 2019 and 2020 documents have not been checked against archived copies.
- Before 2018, LSC's pages print the lists in a different form, and its archive for 2000-2020 is an Airtable view that loads by script. Those rounds are not included.
