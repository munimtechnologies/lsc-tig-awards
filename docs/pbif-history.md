# Pro Bono Innovation Fund 2014-2021, and LSC's running totals checked

Part of [lsc-tig-awards](../README.md) (v0.6.0). Compiled by MV2 of Munim, Inc., with AI agents working in his name. Data and text: CC BY 4.0. Code: Apache-2.0. This is information about public grants. It is not legal advice.

The main README covers LSC's Pro Bono Innovation Fund (PBIF) for 2022-2025, the years on LSC's PBIF Awards page. This page adds the eight earlier rounds (2014-2021), read by hand from LSC's own press releases, and then checks every running total LSC has published for the program against the grants that its releases and its awards page actually name.

## What we found

- **From 2022 on, LSC's own running totals agree exactly with the grants it named.** Counting each grant once, and leaving out eight 2020 supplemental grants that no release names, the lists give exactly LSC's 121 (2022), 139 (2023), 161 (2024) and 180 (2025). LSC's program page says PBIF has funded "180 projects in 38 states, the District of Columbia, and the U.S. Virgin Islands". The lists give the same 180, the same 38 states, DC and the Virgin Islands.
- **The 2020 and 2021 statements are one grant higher, and about $1.1 million higher, than the lists.** The 2020 release says "102 grants totaling more than $27.8 million"; the 2021 release says "$28 million in 94 pro bono projects". The releases name 93 grants for 2014-2020, plus the eight unnamed supplemental grants (101), and their amounts come to $26.88 million. $28 million is exactly the seven funds Congress set aside for 2014-2020 ($2.5M, three times $4M, three times $4.5M), so these two statements look like the money available, not the grants listed. Either one pre-2021 grant is missing from every release we found, or the count was one too high and was corrected in 2022: LSC's 121 equals the 2020-2021 statements' 94 plus 12 for 2021 plus 15 for 2022, but 2021 had 13 grants (Legal Services NYC got two).
- **2014-2017: the grants add up to exactly 95% of each year's fund.** $2,375,000 of $2.5 million in 2014, and $3,800,000 of $4 million in each of 2015, 2016 and 2017, to the dollar. From 2018 the share varies (97.9%, 96.6%, 97.1%). The releases do not say what the other 5% paid for.
- **Two printed totals do not match their own lists.** The 2019 release says $4,347,185, but its 15 grants add up to $4,348,143 ($958 more). The 2020 release repeats the same $4,347,185 for a different round. That round's 11 named grants come to $3,138,716, so if the total is right, the eight supplemental grants come to $1,208,469. The 2021 list comes to $6.97 less than its stated $4,611,052.97 because the release prints whole dollars.
- **Where the money went, 2014-2025.** Twelve states have never had a PBIF grant: Alabama, Arizona, Connecticut, Delaware, Mississippi, North Dakota, New Hampshire, Oklahoma, Oregon, Rhode Island, South Dakota and Wyoming. Neither have American Samoa, Guam, the Micronesia service area or Puerto Rico. Eleven states and DC had grants only before 2022. Indiana, Maryland and Utah are among them: the main README lists them as getting neither TIG nor PBIF in 2021-2025, but each had PBIF grants earlier (Indiana 2016; Maryland 2014 and 2019; Utah 2014). DC, Missouri and Montana, which have high TIG indexes and no 2022-2025 PBIF, had PBIF grants in 2014-2021.
- **A fifth amount difference.** LSC's 2025 California release (as reposted by [Neighborhood Legal Services of Los Angeles County](https://nlsla.org/california-legal-services-providers-receive-1-million-from-lsc-pro-bono-innovation-fund/); LSC's own copy was not found) gives Community Legal Aid SoCal **$346,130**, and its total of $1,053,965 is $346,130 + $344,578 + $363,257, so the release adds up. The PBIF Awards page prints $336,130. The tables here use the page's amount, as the main README does.

## Sources and method

Each round's grants come from LSC's yearly recipients release, saved by a sandbox job on 16 October 2026 and read by hand twice (`data/pbif_history.json` keeps each URL):
[2014](https://www.lsc.gov/press-release/lsc-awards-first-pro-bono-innovation-fund-grants),
[2015](https://www.lsc.gov/press-release/2015-pro-bono-innovation-grant-recipients),
[2016](https://www.lsc.gov/press-release/2016-pro-bono-innovation-grant-recipients),
[2017](https://www.lsc.gov/press-release/2017-pro-bono-innovation-grant-recipients),
[2018](https://www.lsc.gov/press-release/2018-pro-bono-innovation-grant-recipients),
[2019](https://www.lsc.gov/press-release/2019-pro-bono-innovation-grant-recipients),
[2020](https://www.lsc.gov/press-release/2020-pro-bono-innovation-fund-grant-recipients),
[2021](https://www.lsc.gov/press-release/2021-pro-bono-innovation-fund-grant-recipients).
LSC's 2016 and 2017 grant pages give the same amounts as the releases. A grant's state is the state heading LSC printed above it (2016-2021) or the place its release names (2014-2015). Amounts are as printed, in whole dollars.

## Limits

- These are LSC's announcements, not its grant accounting. Amounts can change after a release, and the 2022 and 2023 checks in the main README show the awards page and the releases sometimes disagree.
- We found no release naming the eight 2020 supplemental grants or a 102nd grant before 2021. A search of LSC's site could still turn one up.
- "Through" is the last round a statement can cover, judged from its date. LSC does not say whether its totals count grants or projects; it uses both words.
- An aside, outside PBIF: in the 2017 release, Northwest Justice Project's headline gives its Technology Initiative Grant as $97,750 and the text as $185,250. That release's TIG amounts are not part of this dataset.
