# Pro Bono Innovation Fund dollars per person in poverty, 2014-2025

Part of [lsc-tig-awards](../README.md) (v0.7.0). Compiled by MV2 of Munim, Inc., with AI agents working in his name. Data and text: CC BY 4.0. Code: Apache-2.0. This is information about public grants. It is not legal advice.

LSC divides its main grant money among states by the number of people in poverty. Its Pro Bono Innovation Fund (PBIF) is awarded by competition. This page puts all twelve PBIF rounds from 2014 to 2025 (the 2014-2021 grants of [pbif-history.md](pbif-history.md) and the 2022-2025 awards of the main README) against the people in poverty that LSC funds against, state by state, and asks whether the states that got the most before 2022 kept getting the most after.

## What we found

- **Across 2014-2025, PBIF comes to $1.21 per person in poverty** ($51,070,976.14 over 42,301,792 people in the 56 jurisdictions with basic field money): 72 cents in 2014-2021 and 49 cents in 2022-2025.
- **Vermont got the most: $18.95 per person in poverty**, 15.7 times its share. Then Alaska ($12.36), DC ($11.58), the Virgin Islands ($10.57, from one 2022 grant) and Maine ($6.84).
- **Sixteen jurisdictions never had a PBIF grant in twelve rounds.** Twelve states (Alabama, Arizona, Connecticut, Delaware, Mississippi, North Dakota, New Hampshire, Oklahoma, Oregon, Rhode Island, South Dakota and Wyoming) and four territories (American Samoa, Guam, the Micronesia service area and Puerto Rico). Together they hold 13.3% of the poverty population. Puerto Rico alone holds 3.0%, more than any of the twelve states.
- **Over the twelve rounds, 22 jurisdictions with an index under 0.5 hold 38.0% of the poverty population and received 7.1% of PBIF dollars. The 12 at 2.0 or more hold 11.3% and received 35.6%.** By period: 23 under 0.5 held 32.3% and got 1.9% in 2014-2021, and 32 held 49.6% and got 7.2% in 2022-2025. The 13 at 2.0 or more held 10.1% and got 34.7% before 2022, and 18.5% and 56.0% after.
- **Texas has the largest steady gap.** It holds 10.1% of the poverty population and received 1.9% of PBIF dollars: an index of 0.19 before 2022 and 0.18 after, 23 cents per person in poverty over twelve rounds. Of the four largest poverty populations, California (1.05) and New York (1.30) are near or above their share over the whole span, and Florida (0.44) and Texas are under half.
- **Being at the top before 2022 did not keep a state there.** Six jurisdictions are at 2.0 or more in both periods: Alaska, Iowa, Kentucky, Maine, Vermont and Wisconsin. Of the other seven at 2.0 or more in 2014-2021, five (DC, Hawaii, Missouri, Montana and Nebraska) received no PBIF grant in 2022-2025. The 2022-2025 group adds Kansas, Nevada and the Virgin Islands, which had no grant before 2022, and Illinois, Michigan, Ohio and Virginia, which were under 2.0 before. Across the 56 jurisdictions, the correlation between per-person PBIF dollars in the two periods is 0.28.
- **Being at the bottom mostly did.** 18 jurisdictions are under 0.5 in both periods: the sixteen that never had a grant, plus South Carolina and Texas. Of the 22 with no grant in 2014-2021, six had one in 2022-2025 (Kansas, North Carolina, Nevada, South Carolina, Tennessee and the Virgin Islands).

## Limits

- **One year of poverty figures for twelve rounds.** Every round is set against the poverty population behind LSC's 2025 basic field money (the main README's per-person section). LSC updates its poverty estimates every three years, so a state's share in 2014 was not exactly its 2025 share. Treat the 2014-2021 index as approximate.
- **The eight 2020 supplemental grants are left out** (about $1.2 million if the 2020 release's total is right). No release names them or their states. They went to current grantees, so they could raise some states' 2014-2021 figures but could not add a state to the map.
- Amounts are nominal dollars as LSC printed them, with no adjustment for inflation. Where the 2022-2025 awards page and a release disagree on an amount, the page's amount is used, as in the main README.
- These are awards, not applications. Only LSC grantees can apply, so a state with no grant may have had no applicant, and the data can't tell which. A grant counts once, in the grantee's state, even when the project serves several.
- Native American service areas are not in the people column, because LSC funds them from a fixed set-aside. A PBIF grant to a Native American grantee would still count in its state's dollars.
- A single grant moves a small jurisdiction a long way. The Virgin Islands' 8.76 is one $204,429 grant, and Vermont's 15.70 is four grants. The correlation (0.28) is an unweighted Pearson correlation over the 56 jurisdictions, so a few small jurisdictions with large per-person figures pull it around. Read it as "weak", not as a precise number.
- The 2026 round (announced October 2026, more than $4.15 million to 20 organizations) is not included: LSC's awards page did not list its amounts when this was compiled.
